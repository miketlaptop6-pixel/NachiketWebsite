A resume summary is a concise, powerful statement positioned at the top of your resume, just below the header. It’s meant to capture the attention of hiring managers and is perfectly suited for professionals who want to emphasize their key qualifications, strengths, and accomplishments, effectively conveying their potential impact on the target role.

A well-crafted resume summary is more than just a brief introduction. It's a powerful tool to make your application stand out. An effective summary not only positions you as the ideal candidate but also bridges the gap between your past achievements and future potential, making it an indispensable part of your resume.

In this guide, we're diving into the essentials of crafting resume summaries that will keep recruiters reading. We'll cover how to:

  • Understand the difference between a career summary and an objective statement.
  • Craft your own standout summary using proven, industry-relevant techniques.
  • Tailor your resume summary specifically to the job you're targeting.
  • Enhance your summary with quantifiable achievements for added impact.
  • Draw inspiration from our collection of 100+ powerful summary examples, spanning the most sought-after positions.

What is a summary on a resume and why is it important

The resume summary is a concise career statement that sits prominently beneath the resume header and is one of the first elements recruiters spot during their initial scan of your resume. Essentially, it serves as a bridge connecting you to potential employers. A well-composed summary spans 3-5 short sentences and showcases your most relevant skills and experiences, drawing the reader in and encouraging them to delve deeper into your resume.

Here's why your resume needs a summary:

  • It allows hiring managers to quickly grasp your top skills and the unique contributions you're prepared to make to their team.
  • A good summary allows you to distinguish yourself from the pack and spotlight your personal brand.
  • It's the perfect space to articulate your career aspirations and demonstrate your eagerness to grow within your targeted role.

Resume summary vs objective: which one to choose

While both resume elements serve as a brief introduction to your professional profile, they cater to different stages of a career and have distinct focuses.

Length and purpose

  • Resume summary: Typically consists of 3 to 5 sentences and provides a concise overview of your professional achievements and significant experience. It's designed to capture the employer's attention by showcasing how your background aligns with the job requirements.
  • Objective statement: Generally shorter, with 1 to 3 sentences, an objective statement outlines your career goals and motivation for applying to the position. It is particularly beneficial for first-time job seekers, career changers, and people with gaps in their employment history.

When to use each

  • For emerging professionals: If you're transitioning to a new field or have limited work experience, an objective statement can help clarify your career direction and demonstrate your enthusiasm and potential to grow within the company.
  • For experienced job seekers: Candidates with a solid work history and specific skill sets that match the job description should opt for a resume summary.

Let’s have a look at two examples of a resume summary and an objective for the same position—environmental lawyer.

Lawyer resume objective

OBJECTIVE
Recent law school graduate with a passion for environmental law, eager to apply comprehensive academic knowledge and strong advocacy skills at Green Advocates. Highly motivated to contribute to impactful cases and support the firm's mission of championing sustainable practices and environmental justice.
RIGHT

Even though the candidate has no experience, the objective clearly shows their comprehensive knowledge and direct alignment with the firm’s values and goals.

Lawyer resume summary

SUMMARY
A dependable environmental lawyer with 7 years of experience in environmental litigation and policy advocacy, specializing in climate change laws and renewable energy projects. Led a team that secured a groundbreaking victory in a landmark case reducing carbon emissions by 25% over five years for a major manufacturing plant. Instrumental in negotiating and implementing renewable energy agreements that resulted in a 40% increase in the adoption of sustainable energy sources by local governments and corporations. Expert negotiator and communicator, adept at building strategic partnerships and leading cross-functional teams to achieve common goals and drive positive change.
RIGHT

Unlike the objective, which focuses on aspirations and potential skills, this resume summary highlights concrete achievements and a track record of success in environmental law. This approach reassures potential employers of the lawyer’s ability to handle complex cases and contribute to the firm's goals based on past performance.

The summary goes beyond stating a general interest in environmental law. It showcases specialization in key areas such as climate change laws and renewable energy projects, and highlights leadership skills. Hiring managers will immediately notice the candidate’s readiness to take on significant responsibilities.

When to use a resume summary

Next, let’s focus on the specific use cases when writing a summary is beneficial. How many of the following criteria apply to you?

  1. You have more than 3 years of experience and have honed specific skills that qualify you for the job you’re applying for.
  2. You want to continue working in your current field, establishing a clear career trajectory.
  3. You seek to immediately capture recruiters’ attention by strategically transferring keywords from the job description to your summary statement.
  4. You have in-demand qualifications (such as skills, certifications, and licenses) that should be emphasized early in your resume to secure an interview invite.

Key elements of an effective resume summary

Before delving into the summary writing process, it's crucial to understand its components, which ironically make writing it more challenging. The conciseness required often leaves job seekers struggling to squeeze in all their achievements. Fortunately, they don't need to. By following the guidelines outlined below, you can create a focused and effective summary.

  • Briefness and relevance: Your summary should be adapted to each job application, concisely capturing your most relevant qualifications for the position.
  • Tailoring to the job description: Craft your summary as if responding to a recruiter from your target company asking, "How can you help us succeed?"
  • Highlighting achievements and skills: Prioritize including skills and achievements that are directly relevant to the job you're applying for.
  • Use of keywords for SEO optimization: A careful review of the job listing will reveal the keywords you should incorporate into your resume, helping you pass ATS screenings.

How to write a professional summary for your resume

Mark our words—write the summary last. It will be much easier to do so after you've detailed all your work experience and professional achievements in other resume sections. Plus, you can ask a friend to read the resume and give you feedback about any accomplishments that stand out. You can then select 2-3 of them to highlight in the summary section. From there, do the following:

  1. Study the job posting. Every job description includes a list of requirements, preferred qualifications, and responsibilities that will serve as a checklist for the recruiter to verify if you match their needs.
  2. Consider your current and most relevant experience. Note down every detail that overlaps with the requirements in the job description.
  3. Write 3-5 sentences about your proudest accomplishments. This will essentially form your summary structure. To craft a standout summary, you'll need to provide quantifiable achievements unless you want recruiters to say "So what?" and place you in the rejection pile.

Here's a tested summary template that you can use to write your statement.

Resume summary formula

[Power adjective] [job title] [level of experience] [area of expertise]. [Key skills/ contributions/ achievements/ qualifications] supported by quantifiable results. [How you want to help the prospective employer and how you plan to do it].

Feel free to adjust the following formula to suit your needs and use your unique voice to personalize the summary. After all, you might be competing with hundreds of candidates, but only a few will truly stand out.

Pro tips for a perfect resume summary

Here are some dos and don’ts for crafting a resume summary:

  • Start with an adjective: Choose a powerful adjective that describes you as a professional. Words like dynamic, innovative, resourceful, or dedicated can set the tone for your summary and immediately communicate your work ethic and attitude.
  • Use strong action verbs: To maximize your accomplishments begin each statement with a strong action verb to make your achievements stand out. Verbs, like led, managed, developed, increased, or transformed, showcase your contributions effectively.
  • Include figures and data to prove your statements: Quantifying your achievements with figures, percentages, and data makes your accomplishments concrete and understandable at a glance. For example, "Increased sales by 30% within the first quarter" provides clear evidence of your impact.
  • Avoid personal pronouns: Summaries should be concise and direct, and the best practice is to omit personal pronouns such as "I" or "me."  This style is often recommended to maintain a professional tone and to use space on the resume more efficiently. Using the implied first-person approach, where the pronoun is left out but the verbs suggest the first-person perspective is standard practice.
  • Avoid overused buzzwords: While it’s important to use keywords relevant to the job you’re applying for, steer clear of cliches and buzzwords like hardworking, team player, or go-getter. Instead, focus on specific skills and achievements that demonstrate these qualities.
  • Don’t lie on your resume: Fabricating experiences, skills, or accomplishments can backfire spectacularly, damaging your professional reputation. It's better to focus on your genuine strengths and experiences, framing them in the most positive light possible.

Enhancing your resume summary with keywords

Making your resume stand out to both human eyes and digital systems is crucial. One key strategy is the use of targeted keywords, particularly in your resume summary.

Keywords are specific terms or phrases related to the job you're applying for, including skills, qualifications, software, and industry terminology. They play a significant role in optimizing your resume for Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) and capturing the attention of hiring managers.

Most organizations use ATS to screen resumes, filtering candidates based on relevance to the job requirements. These systems scan your resume for specific keywords that match those found in the job description. Without these keywords, your resume might not pass the initial screening, regardless of your qualifications. Including the right keywords in your resume summary ensures that your application advances to the next stage, increasing your chances of securing an interview.

How to identify and incorporate relevant keywords

  • Analyze the job description: Start by carefully reading the job posting that interests you. Look for frequently mentioned skills, competencies, and qualifications. These are your primary keywords.
  • Use industry-specific terminology: Be aware of the language commonly used in your field. Including industry-specific terms shows your familiarity with the sector. However, be careful not to oversaturate your content with jargon—it still has to be easily understood by non-technical people.
  • Incorporate keywords naturally: While it's important to include keywords, they should fit seamlessly into your summary. It should read naturally, engaging the reader with your professional narrative rather than appearing as a list of keywords.
  • Customize for each application: Tailor your resume summary for each job application. Different employers might prioritize different skills or qualifications, even for similar roles.

How to tailor a resume summary to a job description: example

Review this actual job description for a retail pharmacist position in Hartford, CT. We've highlighted some of the key terms that could be incorporated into the resume summary.

Pharmacist - Hospital Retail

Looking for a motivated individual to support our

  • retail pharmacy operations and serve as a liaison between patients and retail pharmacy for discharge
  • medication prescriptions.

    • Coordinates with providers and patients to ensure smooth discharges and improved medication access for our patients.
    • Ensures the safe and effective use of pharmaceuticals through their proper
    • storage, handling, evaluation, preparation, compounding, labeling, dispensing, and administration in conjunction with state and
    • federal regulations and departmental policies.

    What you will do

    • Prepare, dispense, and supervise the preparation of all medications for patient consumption.
    • Assesses need for formulary items ordered accordingly; initiates contact with prescriber or benefits provider for substitute.
    • Checks for allergies, potential drug-drug interactions/incompatibilities, and adverse drug reactions on all patients before dispensing medication and utilizes appropriate communication channels.
    • Verifies all narcotic orders are received, maintains perpetual inventory log, performs weekly C-II inventory, and conducts monthly controlled drug inventory.
    • Monitors appropriate lab data to ensure patients receive proper dose of medications/nutrition/electrolyte needs when data is available.

    Minimum Qualifications

    • Bachelor’s Degree or Pharm D Degree in Pharmacy.
    • 6-12 months of previous related experience.
    • Licensure required as a Pharmacist by the state of Connecticut.

    • Now, examine the pharmacist’s resume summary. Note that they’ve included their degree and licensure—crucial credentials for any pharmacy professional in the United States.

    Experienced pharmacist with a PharmD degree and license by the State of Connecticut. Streamlined retail pharmacy operations by coordinating with healthcare providers and patients, enhancing medication access and discharge processes, resulting in a 15% increase in patient satisfaction. Optimized pharmaceutical safety and compliance by implementing rigorous evaluation, preparation, and dispensing protocols, reducing medication errors by 20%. Eager to apply proven expertise in C-II inventory management and lab data monitoring to contribute to Meds2Go's pharmacy services.

    The summary integrates a substantial number of keywords from the job description, aligning well with the employer's needs and highlighting the candidate’s relevant skills and achievements.

    While additional keywords from the job description can be (and must be!) woven throughout the resume, the primary goal is to capture the recruiter’s attention within the very first seconds of reviewing the resume.
