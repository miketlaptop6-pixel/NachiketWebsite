What is a cover letter and what does it contain

A cover letter is a one-page document you submit alongside your resume. Its purpose is to introduce you to a potential employer and tell an inspiring story of your career and why you should be their next hire.

A good cover letter can be separated into five different segments: salutation, opener, body, call to action, and closer. When appropriate, you can cover any gaps in employment, your availability, and if you’re ready to relocate.

Now, let’s dig deeper into the sections that a good cover letter includes.

Address

Share your own contact details along with the recipient's contact information.

Opening paragraph/Salutation

Introduce yourself and state the purpose of your letter. The salutation shows respect to the recipient and is tailored to the individual, if possible, to add a personal touch.

Body paragraph(s)

Elaborate on your qualifications, experience, and skills related to the job. This section is the core of your cover letter, offering compelling evidence of why you're the ideal candidate for the position.

Closing

Reiterate your interest, sign-off, and request a follow-up.

Professional Cover Letter Examples

A tech recruiter looks for completely different metrics than a healthcare administrator. To get past the ATS and impress the hiring manager, your application needs to speak their language. General cover letter example

txt
Image

Elizabeth Lee | Strategic Analyst | B2B Pricing & Revenue Growth

212-555-0649 | elizabeth.lee@NachiketBhogawar.in.com | New York, NY

September 5, 2024

Bain & Company

415 Mission Street, Suite 4800

San Francisco, California 94105

Dear Ms. Victoria Andres,

During my recent time at Kaiser Associates, I built a custom pricing model that increased B2B sales by 14% for a Fortune 100 client. I am eager to bring this same data-driven approach to the Associate Consultant position at Bain & Company's San Francisco office.

As an analyst, I specialize in finding hidden market opportunities. Recently, I evaluated the pricing strategies of competing manufacturers by gathering and cleaning complex data from several disconnected departments. I presented my findings using interactive Tableau dashboards, which secured immediate executive approval for a pricing adjustment that generated $1.2M in new revenue within six months.

Beyond raw data analysis, I pride myself on turning complex numbers into actionable strategies for our staff. For example, I recently designed and led a data strategy seminar in Beijing for 15 regional managers. By customizing the training to fit their local market challenges, we saw a 20% increase in software adoption across the Asian market.

With my proven track record of turning data into measurable financial growth, I am confident I can make an immediate impact on your consulting team. I look forward to discussing my candidacy with you soon. Thank you for your time.

Sincerely,

Elizabeth Lee

Why this is a great general cover letter example:

This template uses a highly versatile structure that works for almost any industry because it focuses on proven value rather than just listing daily tasks.

  • It opens with a "hook": Instead of a boring intro, it immediately grabs the hiring manager's attention with a major, quantifiable achievement.
  • It proves business impact: It backs up skills with hard numbers (e.g., a 14% increase in sales and $1.2M in new revenue).
  • It balances hard and soft skills: It effectively showcases technical expertise (data analysis) alongside strong communication and leadership abilities.

ELENA RODRIGUEZ | MULTIDISCIPLINARY SENIOR ART DIRECTOR

VISUAL STORYTELLING & CAMPAIGN STRATEGY

Brooklyn, NY 11201 | (555) 019-8372 | elena.rodriguez@email.com | Portfolio: elenarodriguez.design

April 23, 2026

Karl Lieberman

Chief Creative Officer

Wieden+Kennedy New York

150 Varick St

New York, NY 10013

Dear Karl Lieberman,

When Wieden+Kennedy launched the "Feel Your All" campaign for Nike, the strategic audacity to let raw emotion drive the art direction stood out. As an Art Director who believes design must provoke a reaction before communicating a message, I am applying for the Senior Art Director role at Wieden+Kennedy New York.

During my four years at Droga5, I have specialized in translating complex brand narratives into culturally relevant visual campaigns. I use design systems to solve business problems and drive measurable action.

A few key highlights of my recent impact include:

Engagement Growth: Spearheaded the visual refresh for Paramount+'s social ecosystem, resulting in a 42% increase in engagement and 1.2 million new followers within six months.

Budget Efficiency: Managed a tight $150,000 production budget for an integrated campaign, utilizing smart asset repurposing to deliver the project 15% under budget.

Cross-Functional Leadership: Directed 5 junior creatives, increasing our pitch win-rate by 25% year-over-year through stronger conceptual alignment.

Technically, my process prioritizes speed and precision. I am highly proficient in Figma, After Effects, Cinema 4D, and Adobe Creative Cloud. Recently, I integrated AI tools like Midjourney into early-stage storyboarding, cutting our concepting phase time by a third while elevating creative output.

I am eager to bring my data-conscious strategy and relentless execution to W+K's iconic brands. My portfolio is available at elenarodriguez.design.

Thank you for your consideration. I look forward to discussing how my vision aligns with Wieden+Kennedy’s upcoming projects.

Best regards,

Elena Rodriguez

Why this is a great creative cover letter example:

  • Visual Professionalism: The bolded contact line creates a clear visual anchor, immediately establishing your identity as a high-level creative professional.
  • Scannable Performance Metrics: By isolating key achievements like the 42% engagement increase and budget savings into their own paragraphs, you ensure that high-impact data is not lost in a dense block of text.
  • Technical Proficiency: The structure allows industry-specific tools like Figma and Cinema 4D to be easily identified by hiring managers scanning for technical requirements.
  • Strategic Flow: Using simple paragraph tags maintains a clean, modern aesthetic that reflects the organized and precise nature of an Art Director's workflow.

DAVID CHEN

INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGNER | CURRICULUM DEVELOPMENT & ADULT LEARNING

Chicago, IL 60605 | (555) 348-9102 | david.chen@NachiketBhogawar.in.com | Portfolio: davidchen.learning

April 23, 2026

Nathalie Scardino

Chief People Officer

Salesforce

Salesforce Tower, 415 Mission St

San Francisco, CA 94105

Dear Nathalie Scardino,

Salesforce’s Trailhead platform proves that enterprise training can be both rigorous and deeply engaging. As an educator transitioning into corporate enablement, I have spent seven years mastering how people retain complex information. I am applying for the Instructional Designer position on the Global Enablement team to bring that expertise to your enterprise users.

As Lead Educator for Chicago Public Schools, my daily reality has been that of a full-stack instructional designer. I am not simply shifting careers; I am shifting audiences, offering a unique advantage for Salesforce's learning objectives:

Translating Complexity at Scale: Corporate enablement requires making dense technical updates digestible. I designed a district-wide digital curriculum utilized by over 1,200 users. By applying cognitive load theory and scaffolding techniques, this initiative drove a 28% increase in proficiency scores over two years.

Driving Adult Learning & Adoption: System adoption is just as critical as the training itself. During our organization's transition to a complex new Learning Management System (LMS), I developed and led targeted professional development workshops for 45 peers, ultimately reducing new-technology onboarding time by 30%.

Technical Readiness & Execution: I bridge the gap between pedagogy and digital execution by strictly adhering to the ADDIE and SAM models. Furthermore, my portfolio (davidchen.learning) demonstrates my immediate, hands-on proficiency in corporate e-learning stacks, specifically Articulate Storyline 360, TechSmith Camtasia, and enterprise LMS administration.

I am eager to bring my data-backed approach to adult learning to Salesforce. Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to discussing how my non-traditional background is a strategic asset for your enablement goals.

Best regards,

David Chen

Why this is a good career change cover letter

  • Highlighting Transferable Skills: By using clean paragraph breaks, you can clearly bridge the gap between your past experience and your new industry, making the connection obvious for the recruiter.
  • Professional Authority: Even if you are new to the field, a structured and polished HTML layout demonstrates the soft skills—like organization and attention to detail—that are valuable in any role.
  • Optimized for Email: This structure is perfect for an email cover letter or for sending your cover letter in an email, as the spacing remains consistent across different platforms and devices.
  • Narrative Clarity: Breaking your story into distinct sections helps the hiring manager follow your "why" without getting lost in a long, confusing explanation of your previous background.
  • Technical Visibility: By isolating software tools and methodology keywords (ADDIE, Articulate 360) in their own blocks, you prove technical readiness despite the non-traditional career path.

6 Tips for Writing a Strong Cover Letter

A cover letter accompanies your resume to show your personality and explain your qualifications in a way bullet points simply can't. But staring at a blank page can feel daunting. If you look closely at the successful examples in our directory, you'll notice they all rely on the same winning framework. Keep these six proven tips in mind to ensure your cover letter introduces the candidate effectively, bypasses the ATS, and actively supports your job application.
Tailor It to the Job
Hiring managers can spot a generic, copy-pasted letter from a mile away. To stand out, ensure that your strong cover letter is tailored to the job description. Use the exact keywords found in the job posting to prove you are a perfect match for that specific role.
Highlight Measurable Achievements
Do not waste space listing basic daily duties. Instead, ensure your strong cover letter highlights measurable achievements. Use hard data, percentages, and specific metrics to prove exactly what you accomplished for your previous employer.
Avoid Repeating Your Resume
Your letter should expand on your resume, not just regurgitate it. A strong cover letter connects your skills with job requirements by telling a compelling, detailed story about one or two specific wins, rather than giving a broad overview of your entire career.
Focus on Clean Cover Letter Design
Hiring managers are incredibly busy, and a cluttered page will get skipped. A strong cover letter uses a professional tone not just in its writing, but in its visual layout. Explore our guide on cover letter design to ensure you are using enough white space and clean typography to make your narrative visually appealing.
Show Enthusiasm for the Company
Don't just talk about what the job will do for you—talk about what you can do for the company. A strong cover letter demonstrates company knowledge by referencing recent projects, core values, or industry news, proving your genuine interest in their specific mission.
Include a Clear Call-to-Action
A passive sign-off rarely wins the interview. Notice how an effective cover letter closing includes a call to action. Instead of just saying "thanks," our top-performing samples confidently request a brief call or meeting with the hiring manager to discuss how their specific background aligns with the team's upcoming goals.

Common cover letter mistakes to avoid

Use a real name: A massive cover letter mistake is using a generic greeting (which shows zero personalization).

Tell a new story: A common cover letter mistake is repeating your resume (avoid resume repetition at all costs).

Keep it short: A rookie cover letter mistake is writing more than one page (always respect the hiring manager's preferred length).

Make it about them: A guaranteed cover letter mistake is failing to tailor to the job requirements.

Run a spell-check: The most preventable cover letter mistake is including spelling errors.

Cover Letter Examples FAQs

How long should a cover letter be? Is 500 words too long?
Yes, 500 words is generally too long. When determining your cover letter length, the golden rule is that a cover letter length is one page. Keep it between 250 and 400 words so the hiring manager can quickly scan your best points.
Is a cover letter still necessary?
Yes. While candidates often debate cover letter necessity, a cover letter is required for a job application in almost all professional scenarios unless the job ad explicitly tells you not to include one. Submitting a complete job application gives you a massive advantage over applicants who only send a resume.
What are the 3 things a cover letter must include?
While templates vary, every standard three-to-four-paragraph cover letter must include: • An engaging introduction (the hook) that names the target role. • A body section that highlights measurable achievements. • A strong closing statement with a clear call to action.
Who do you address a cover letter to if there is no name?
Whenever possible, your cover letter is addressed to the hiring manager directly. If you absolutely cannot find the exact name of the hiring manager after researching the company website or LinkedIn, address it to the specific department (e.g., "Dear Marketing Department" or "Dear Customer Service Team").
How do I write a cover letter for my first job with no experience?
If you have no direct work experience, focus on your transferable skills. Highlight academic projects, volunteer work, extracurricular leadership, or internships. Employers hiring for entry-level roles are looking for enthusiasm, a willingness to learn, and strong soft skills.
Can I use ChatGPT to write my cover letter?
You can use AI tools to outline or brainstorm, but you should never submit a 100% AI-generated letter. Hiring managers can spot robotic phrasing and generic templates. If you use AI, heavily edit the output to inject your own voice, specific metrics, and genuine enthusiasm.
Should my cover letter just repeat what is on my resume?
No, you want to avoid direct resume overlap. A cover letter complements your resume—it doesn't just repeat the timeline. Instead, your cover letter explains your resume experience by diving into the specific context, challenges, and impact of your biggest wins.
Should I address an employment gap in my cover letter?

If you think that addressing your employment gap is important, then definitely include a sentence about it. With that being said, employment gaps aren’t uncommon and most recruiters would love to know more about your unemployment gap as it can help them understand more about you.

Should I include information about my availability?

Of course! Cover letters are meant to help clear up any questions that your resume may not. Adding a sentence to the body segment of your motivation letter is a great way to do so.

Can I mention that I’m fine with relocating for the job?

Yes. Oftentimes, cover letters are the perfect place to mention that you’re okay with moving cities or even states if you get the job. Resumes generally only give you the opportunity to mention where you live, but not that you’re open to relocating.

Should I mention anything about the salary in the cover letter?

This is a bit of a double-edged sword. If the salary for the position is included in the job ad, and you’re okay with it, then yes, you can say that in the cover letter. Otherwise, don’t talk in the cover letter about the salary. Save it for the first or second interview instead.
